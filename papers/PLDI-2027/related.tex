\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

The introduction discusses the work closest to ours. This section adds
compiler proofs grouped by semantic style, further operational proofs
of closure conversion, relations between denotations, graph models,
and comparisons of semantic styles.

\paragraph{Semantic styles in compiler correctness}

The idea that a compiler is correct when a diagram of meanings commutes
goes back to \citet{Morris:1973ab} and \citet{Thatcher:1981aa}, and
\citet{Milne:1976aa} related semantics at different levels of
abstraction with inclusive predicates. Among proofs of particular
compilers, those of \citet{Pardo:2018aa} and \citet{Siek:2017ab} are
denotational for both the source and the target of each pass.
\citet{Pardo:2018aa} give clocked denotations to a first-order
imperative language and a stack language, and \citet{Siek:2017ab}
proves an inliner correct by equality of graph-model denotations. Other
proofs mix the styles, with a denotational semantics on only one side.
\citet{Benton:2009aa} relate a domain-theoretic semantics of a typed
language with recursion to an operational SECD machine, using
step-indexed biorthogonal relations; the target machine has primitive
closures. \OE{}uf \citep{Mullen:2018aa} gives a denotation only to its
total source language and proves every pass operationally. Most
compiler proofs, finally, are operational on both sides. The CLI stack
\citep{Moore:1989aa,Young:1989aa} runs the source for $n$ steps and the
target for a number of steps given by a clock function, and shows that the target reproduces every run of the source that ends without error, but says nothing about divergence.
CompCert \citep{Leroy:2009aa} and CakeML \citep{Kumar:2014aa},
discussed in the introduction, use simulations and clocked big-step
semantics, respectively. The calculated compilers of
\citet{Bahr:2015aa} derive a compiler and its virtual machine from a
definitional interpreter; the virtual machine keeps closures as values.
Our back end adapts the operational proofs of \citet{Siek:2025ab} for a
first-order compiler.

\paragraph{Operational proofs of closure conversion}

Typed closure conversion represents a closure as a package of an
existential type, as our FlatClos does. \citet{Minamide:1996ys} prove
it correct with logical relations between source and target terms,
\citet{Ahmed:2008aa} show that it preserves observational equivalence,
and \citet{Bowman:2018aa} extend it to the calculus of constructions.
\citet{Pottier:2006aa} prove that defunctionalization preserves
convergence and divergence with a simulation. The simulation proof of \citet{Wand:1994aa} covers only terminating
programs, and \citet{Neis:2015aa} use parametric inter-language
simulations.

\paragraph{Relations between denotations}

Relating two denotational semantics of one language is an old problem.
Our Scott-domain relation for \pass{delay}
(Section~\ref{sec:scott-delay}) follows \citet{Reynolds:1974aa}, who
related the direct and continuation semantics of a language by a
relation built stage by stage along the inverse-limit construction of
the domains \citep{Scott:1971aa}. His index ranges over the stages of
both domains, and his relation is the intersection over all stages.
Ours recurses on the stages of one side only and relates each
approximation to the whole other value. The approximant logical
relation (Section~\ref{sec:approx-lr}) has a similar shape, but its
index bounds the depth of finite elements of the target side, and
depth irrelevance (Lemma~\ref{lem:depth-irrelevant}) makes an
intersection unnecessary. \citet{Nielsen:2000aa} relates the
denotational meanings of a program and its defunctionalization with a
type-indexed relation whose function clause, like that of our
preservation relation, relates a function to a closure whose calls
give related results. His source has no recursive types, so recursion
on types suffices, and he proves only preservation. Later work related
two semantics with inclusive predicates
\citep{Milne:1976aa,Stoy:1977aa}, finitary projections
\citep{Mulmuley:1987aa}, or types \citep{Meyer:1985aa,Filinski:2007aa}.
\citet{Pitts:1996aa} showed that a relation specified by a
mixed-variance equation over a recursively defined domain exists and
is unique, but his method does not apply directly to \pass{delay}
(Section~\ref{sec:scott-delay}). Adequacy proofs, which relate a
denotation to syntax, often index a relation by finite elements
\citep{Winskel:1993aa,Abramsky:1990vv,Siek:2017ab}. Step-indexed
\citep{Appel:2001aa,Ahmed:2006aa} and guarded
\citep{Mogelberg:2016aa} relations are the operational and synthetic
alternatives. To our knowledge, the approximant logical relation is
the first relation between two denotational semantics indexed by
finite approximations on one side only.

\paragraph{Graph models and consistency}

Our model descends from the graph models of \citet{Plotkin:1993ab},
\citet{Scott:1976lq}, and \citet{Engeler:1981aa} and is related to the
filter models of \citet{Barendregt:1983aa}. Our consistency is
pairwise, as in the coherent domains of \citet{Plotkin:1978aa}, and our
rule for entries is the function-space consistency of information
systems \citep{Scott:1982aa,Larsen:1984aa} in the pairwise form of
\citet{Schwichtenberg:2006aa}.

\paragraph{Comparisons of semantic styles}

\citet{Leroy:2009rc} and \citet{Danielsson:2012aa} prove compilers correct with coinductive big-step semantics and with the partiality monad, and \citet{Knothe:2026aa} combine small-step and big-step semantics within CompCert; none includes a denotational semantics or a whole compiler for a higher-order language.
