\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our main result is a denotational proof that closure conversion
preserves and reflects the meaning of programs, divergence included,
for a language with general recursion. Reflection is the hard
direction. A relation between single elements cannot prove it, because
a target call takes its code and its environment from different pairs,
and a relation between whole denotations has nothing to recurse on in
the function case. The approximant logical relation relates a finite
list of target elements, which contains both halves of every call it
describes, to the whole source denotation, and recurses on the depth of
that list. With it, the graph-model proof of a ten-pass compiler,
mechanized in Agda, consists of short equational calculations for the
nine passes that keep the representation of values and one relational
proof for \pass{delay}, which is several times the size of the
operational proofs of that pass. In future work we plan to report on a
dynamically typed variant of the compiler and to repeat the comparison
in Rocq or Lean.
