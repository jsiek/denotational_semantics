\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our main results are denotational proofs, one for a graph model and
another for Scott domains, that closure conversion preserves and
reflects the meaning of programs, divergence included, for a
Turing-complete language. The proof of semantic reflection for the
graph model rests on our new \emph{approximant logical relation}.  We
extend the proofs to an eight-pass compiler and mechanize them in
Agda.  Comparing the size of these proofs to operational proofs of the
same compiler shows a split by the kind of pass. Where a pass keeps
the representation of values, its denotational proof is an equation
between meanings, and the graph-model proofs are smaller than the
machine and reduction proofs. For the source optimizer, the
graph-model proof is the smallest of the five. Where a pass changes
that representation, as \pass{split closure} does, proving semantic
reflection requires a relation between denotations, and the
operational proofs, which follow the run of the target and get
reflection from determinism, are several times smaller.  Not counting
infrastructure, the clocked interpreters give the smallest proof, the
graph model the second, and Scott domains the fourth.
