\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proved a ten-pass compiler correct, from a call-by-value
$\lambda$-calculus with recursive types down to a typed assembly
language, using a graph-model denotational semantics for every
language, and mechanized the proof in Agda (RQ1). Nine of the ten
passes are proved by short equational calculations (RQ2). The
exception is \pass{delay}, the step of closure conversion that lowers
closures to pairs of code and environment: its correctness is not an
equation, and its reflection proof needs a new kind of relation, the
approximant logical relation, defined by recursion on the depth of a
finite approximation of the target (RQ3). Among three operational
proofs of the same compiler, the denotational proof ranks second in
size: it tends to be the smallest, or close to it, for the equational
passes, and much larger for \pass{delay} (RQ4).
A second denotational proof, with Scott domains, is larger, and its
proof of \pass{delay} follows Reynolds rather than Pitts. In future
work we plan to report on a dynamically typed variant of the compiler
and to repeat the comparison in Rocq or Lean.
