\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our main results are denotational proofs, for two models, that closure
conversion preserves and reflects the meaning of programs, divergence
included, for a language with general recursion; we extend the proofs
to a ten-pass compiler down to assembly and mechanize them in Agda.
Comparing to operational proofs of the same compiler shows a split by
the kind of pass. Where a pass keeps the representation of values, its
denotational proof is an equation between meanings, and the
graph-model proofs tend to be smaller than or close to the operational
ones; for the source optimizer, the graph-model proof is the smallest
of the five. Where a pass changes that representation, as \pass{delay}
does, reflection needs a relation between denotations, and
the operational proofs, which follow the run of the target and get
reflection from determinism, are several times smaller.  Not counting
infrastructure, the clocked interpreters give the smallest proof, the
graph model the second, and Scott domains the fourth.
