\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our main result is a denotational proof that closure conversion
preserves and reflects the meaning of programs, divergence included,
for a language with general recursion. For reflection, we introduced
the approximant logical relation, which relates a finite list of target
elements to the whole source denotation and recurses on the depth of
that list. With it, the graph-model proof of a ten-pass compiler,
mechanized in Agda, consists of short equational calculations for the
nine passes that keep the representation of values and one relational
proof for the step of closure conversion that lowers closures to pairs
of code and environment, which is several times the size of the
operational proofs of that step. In future work we plan to report on a
dynamically typed variant of the compiler and to repeat the comparison
in Rocq or Lean.
