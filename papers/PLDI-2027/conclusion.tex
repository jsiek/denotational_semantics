\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proved a ten-pass compiler correct, from a call-by-value
$\lambda$-calculus with recursive types down to a typed assembly
language, using a graph-model denotational semantics for every
language, and mechanized the proof in Agda (RQ1). Nine of the ten
passes are proved by an equation between denotations, with short
calculations whose remaining cases are congruences (RQ2). The
exception is \pass{delay}, the step of closure conversion that lowers
closures to pairs of code and environment: its correctness is not an
equation, and its reflection proof needs a new kind of relation, the
approximant logical relation, defined by recursion on the depth of a
finite approximation of the target (RQ3). Compared with three
operational proofs of the same compiler, the denotational proof ranks
second in size: it tends to be the smallest, or close to it, for the
passes proved by an equation, and much larger for \pass{delay} (RQ4).

Several directions remain. The ISWIM pipeline in our development also
compiles a dynamically typed source language with run-time errors,
which the graph model can express with an error element; we plan to
report that comparison separately. A proof of the same compiler with
Scott domains would show how much of the cost of \pass{delay} is due to
the graph model, and whether the relational properties of recursively
defined domains \citep{Pitts:1996aa} make reflection easier. Finally,
the comparison could be repeated in a proof assistant with more
automation, such as Rocq or Lean.
