\section{The Graph Model}
\label{app:graph-model}

\subsection{Consistency}
\label{app:consistency}

Consistency is defined on elements, and from it on sets and lists
(Section~\ref{sec:graph-models}).  Two constants are consistent when
they are equal, two entries are consistent when consistent inputs lead
to consistent outputs, and elements that describe different kinds of
values, such as $\mathsf{left}(u)$ and $\mathsf{right}(v)$, are not
consistent. A set $D$ is consistent, written $\mathsf{con}(D)$, when
its elements are pairwise consistent, and two lists are consistent
with each other, written $V \approx V'$, when every element of one is
consistent with every element of the other.

\begin{figure}[h]
\small\figdefs
\defhead{Consistency of elements}{u \sim v}
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  c \sim c' & \iff & c = c'\\
  (V \mapsto w) \sim (V' \mapsto w') & \iff &
    (V \approx V' \Rightarrow w \sim w')
    \qquad
    (V \mapsto w) \sim \nu, \quad \nu \sim \nu\\
  \pairv{u}{v} \sim \pairv{u'}{v'} & \iff & u \sim u' \land v \sim v'\\
  \mathsf{tup}_i(v) \sim \mathsf{tup}_j(v') & \iff & i = j \Rightarrow v \sim v'\\
  \mathsf{left}(v) \sim \mathsf{left}(v') & \iff & v \sim v'
    \qquad
  \mathsf{right}(v) \sim \mathsf{right}(v') \iff v \sim v'
\end{array}
\]
\noindent and no other pairs of elements are consistent.

\defhead{Consistency of sets and lists}{\mathsf{con}(D) \qquad V \approx V'}
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \mathsf{con}(D) & \iff & u \sim v \text{ for all } u, v \in D\\
  V \approx V' & \iff & u \sim v \text{ for all } u \in V \text{ and } v \in V'
\end{array}
\]
\caption{Consistency of elements, sets, and lists.}
\label{fig:consistency}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Depth}
\label{app:depth}

The depth of an element of the graph model is the height of its
syntax tree, not counting constants, $\nu$ and $\langle\rangle$; the depth of a finite list is the largest depth of its
elements (zero for the empty list):
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \depth(V \mapsto w) & = & 1 + \max(\depth(V), \depth(w))\\
  \depth(\pairv{u}{v}) & = & 1 + \max(\depth(u), \depth(v))\\
  \depth(t(v)) & = & 1 + \depth(v)
    \quad \text{for } t \in \{\mathsf{tup}_i, \mathsf{left}, \mathsf{right}\}\\
  \depth(v) & = & 0
    \quad \text{for } v \in \{c, \nu, \langle\rangle\}\\
  \depth(V) & = & \max_{v \in V} \depth(v)
\end{array}
\]

\subsection{Further Operators}
\label{app:operators}

%% Framework/GraphModel/Ops.agda (𝒞, guard-n, strict), OpsBase.agda (ℬ),
%% Semantics/Graph/Prim.agda (primD, out), Language/Prim.agda (δ).
Besides the operators of Figure~\ref{fig:graph-ops}, the semantics of
the languages use the following ones. The empty tuple means
$\mathcal{T}() = \{\langle\rangle\}$. A primitive applies to the integers
in its operands; $\kw{eq}$ produces $\mathsf{left}(\langle\rangle)$ for
true and $\mathsf{right}(\langle\rangle)$ for false:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \mathsf{prim}_p(D, E) & = & \{\, \mathit{out}(\delta(p, n, m)) \mid n \in D,\; m \in E \text{ integers} \,\}\\
  \delta(\kw{sub}, n, m) & = & n - m
  \qquad
  \delta(\kw{eq}, n, m) = (n = m)\\
  \mathit{out}(n) & = & n
  \qquad
  \mathit{out}(\mathsf{true}) = \mathsf{left}(\langle\rangle)
  \qquad
  \mathit{out}(\mathsf{false}) = \mathsf{right}(\langle\rangle)
\end{array}
\]
The operator $\mathcal{C}$ gives the meaning of a $\kw{case}$. Like
$\Lambda$, it binds only nonempty, consistent, finite lists, here of
the payloads of the injections in the scrutinee:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \mathcal{C}(D, F, G) & = &
    \{\, w \mid V \neq [\,],\; \mathsf{con}(V),\; \mathsf{left}(V) \subseteq D,\; w \in F(V) \,\}\\
  & \cup & \{\, w \mid V \neq [\,],\; \mathsf{con}(V),\; \mathsf{right}(V) \subseteq D,\; w \in G(V) \,\}
\end{array}
\]
where $\mathsf{left}(V) = \{\mathsf{left}(v) \mid v \in V\}$, and
likewise for $\mathsf{right}$. Finally, a guard makes a set strict in
other sets:
\[
  \mathsf{guard}(D_1, \ldots, D_n;\, E) = E
  \text{ if } D_1, \ldots, D_n \text{ are nonempty, and } \emptyset \text{ otherwise.}
\]

\section{The Languages}
\label{app:languages}
\label{app:types}

%% Typing: definitions.tex (Types and Languages) and
%% agda/Mu/Language/*.agda. Semantics: agda/Mu/Semantics/Graph/*.agda.
This appendix gives, for each language of Section~\ref{sec:compiler},
its type system and its semantics in the graph model. The base types
are $\kw{Nat}$, $\kw{Int}$, $\kw{Bool}$, $\kw{Unit}$, and $\kw{Void}$.
The result type $\mathit{out}(p)$ of a primitive is $\kw{Int}$ for
$\kw{sub}$ and $\kw{tup}() + \kw{tup}()$ for $\kw{eq}$. 

In the semantics, an environment $\rho$ maps each variable to a set of
elements, and $\rho[x{:=}X]$ maps $x$ to $X$ and every other variable
as $\rho$ does. Every position of an environment must be nonempty, so
the environment $\rho_0$ of a closed program maps every variable to
$\{\langle\rangle\}$, and so do the positions of an environment that
hold no variable. The meaning of a closed program $M$ is
$\den{M} = \den{M}\rho_0$. The operators are those of
Figure~\ref{fig:graph-ops} and Appendix~\ref{app:operators}.

\subsection{The \iswim\ Language}

A judgment $\Gamma \vdash M : A$ types a term in a context $\Gamma$ of
variable types.
\[
\begin{array}{@{}c@{}}
  \dfrac{x{:}A \in \Gamma}{\Gamma \vdash x : A}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma, x{:}A \vdash N : C}{\Gamma \vdash \lambda x.\, N : A \rightarrow C}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash L : A \rightarrow C \quad \Gamma \vdash M : A}{\Gamma \vdash L\;M : C}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{c \text{ a constant of base type } B}{\Gamma \vdash c : B}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M_i : A_i \ (1 \leq i \leq n)}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{tuple}\;(M_1, \ldots, M_n) : \kw{tup}(A_1, \ldots, A_n)}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M : \kw{tup}(A_1, \ldots, A_n) \quad 1 \leq i \leq n}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{get}_i\,M : A_i}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M : A}{\Gamma \vdash \kw{inl}\;M : A + C}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M : C}{\Gamma \vdash \kw{inr}\;M : A + C}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash L : A + C \quad \Gamma, x{:}A \vdash M : D \quad \Gamma, y{:}C \vdash N : D}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{case}\;L\;(x.\,M)\;(y.\,N) : D}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash L : \kw{Int} \quad \Gamma \vdash M : \kw{Int}}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{prim}_p\;L\;M : \mathit{out}(p)}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M : A \quad \Gamma, x{:}A \vdash N : C}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{let}\;x = M\;\kw{in}\;N : C}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M : A[\mu X.\, A / X]}{\Gamma \vdash \kw{fold}\;M : \mu X.\, A}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M : \mu X.\, A}{\Gamma \vdash \kw{unfold}\;M : A[\mu X.\, A / X]}
\end{array}
\]
The semantics:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\qquad}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \den{x}\rho & = & \rho(x) &
  \den{c}\rho & = & \{c\}\\
  \den{\lambda x.\, N}\rho & = & \Lambda(\mlam{X} \den{N}\rho[x{:=}X]) &
  \den{L\;M}\rho & = & \den{L}\rho \app \den{M}\rho\\
  \den{\kw{tuple}\;(M_1, \ldots, M_n)}\rho & = & \mathcal{T}(\den{M_1}\rho, \ldots, \den{M_n}\rho) &
  \den{\kw{get}_i\,M}\rho & = & \pi_{\mathsf{tup}_i}(\den{M}\rho)\\
  \den{\kw{inl}\;M}\rho & = & \mathsf{inl}(\den{M}\rho) &
  \den{\kw{inr}\;M}\rho & = & \mathsf{inr}(\den{M}\rho)\\
  \den{\kw{prim}_p\;L\;M}\rho & = & \mathsf{prim}_p(\den{L}\rho, \den{M}\rho) &
  \den{\kw{fold}\;M}\rho & = & \den{M}\rho\\
  \den{\kw{let}\;x = M\;\kw{in}\;N}\rho & = & \Lambda(\mlam{X} \den{N}\rho[x{:=}X]) \app \den{M}\rho &
  \den{\kw{unfold}\;M}\rho & = & \den{M}\rho
\end{array}
\]
\[
  \den{\kw{case}\;L\;(x.\,M)\;(y.\,N)}\rho =
    \mathcal{C}(\den{L}\rho,\; \mlam{X} \den{M}\rho[x{:=}X],\; \mlam{Y} \den{N}\rho[y{:=}Y])
\]

\subsection{The Clos and EnvClos Languages}

Clos and EnvClos type and interpret their other forms as \iswim\ does.
A closure's type does not show the types $\Delta = A_1, \ldots, A_n$ of
its captured values.
\[
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M_i : A_i \ (1 \leq i \leq n) \qquad
         x_1{:}A_1, \ldots, x_n{:}A_n, y{:}A \vdash N : C}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{clos}\;(\vec{x}, y.\,N)\;\vec{M} : A \rightarrow C}\;\text{(Clos)}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash M_i : A_i \ (1 \leq i \leq n) \qquad
         z{:}\kw{tup}(\Delta),\, y{:}A \vdash N : C}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{clos}\;(z, y.\,N)\;\vec{M} : A \rightarrow C}\;\text{(EnvClos)}
\]
Below, $\den{\vec{M}}\rho$ stands for $\den{M_1}\rho, \ldots, \den{M_n}\rho$. A Clos closure is the function of its parameter $y$ whose code runs
with the captured values for $x_1, \ldots, x_n$; closures are strict, so
it is guarded by the captured values. An EnvClos closure applies the
curried meaning of its code to the tuple of the captured values
(Section~\ref{sec:approx-lr}).
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
    \den{\kw{clos}\;(\vec{x}, y.\,N)\;\vec{M}}\rho =
    \mathsf{guard}(\den{M_1}\rho, \ldots, \den{M_n}\rho;\;
      \Lambda(\mlam{Y} \den{N}\rho_0[\vec{x}{:=}\den{\vec{M}}\rho, y{:=}Y]))
    \hfill \text{(Clos)}\\[4pt]
    \den{\kw{clos}\;(z, y.\,N)\;\vec{M}}\rho =
    \Lambda(\mlam{X} \Lambda(\mlam{Y} \den{N}\rho_0[z{:=}X, y{:=}Y])) \app
      \mathcal{T}(\den{\vec{M}}\rho)
    \quad \text{(EnvClos)}
\end{array}
\]

\subsection{The FlatClos Language}

FlatClos replaces closures and application with code, packing, and
calls, and types and interprets its other forms as \iswim\ does:
\[
  \dfrac{z{:}\kw{tup}(\Delta),\, y{:}A \vdash N : C}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N) : \kw{code}(\Delta; A \rightarrow C)}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash L : \kw{code}(\Delta; A \rightarrow C)
         \quad \Gamma \vdash M : \kw{tup}(\Delta)}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{pack}\;L\;M : A \rightarrow C}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Gamma \vdash L : A \rightarrow C \quad \Gamma \vdash M : A}
        {\Gamma \vdash \kw{callc}\;L\;M : C}
\]
\[
  \den{\kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N)}\rho =  \Lambda(\mlam{X} \Lambda(\mlam{Y} \den{N}\rho_0[z{:=}X, y{:=}Y]))
  \qquad
  \den{\kw{pack}\;L\;M}\rho = \mathsf{pair}(\den{L}\rho, \den{M}\rho)
\]
\[
  \den{\kw{callc}\;L\;M}\rho = \den{L}\rho \callop \den{M}\rho
\]

\subsection{The GlobalFun and Mon Languages}

A GlobalFun program has a table $\Sigma = \sigma_0, \ldots, \sigma_{m-1}$
of the signatures $\sigma_k = (\Delta_k; A_k \rightarrow C_k)$ of its
definitions, and a term is typed by the table as well as its context,
$\Sigma; \Gamma \vdash M : A$. The other forms are typed as in FlatClos.
\[
  \dfrac{k < m}{\Sigma; \Gamma \vdash f_k : \kw{code}(\Delta_k; A_k \rightarrow C_k)}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Sigma; z{:}\kw{tup}(\Delta_k), y{:}A_k \vdash N_k : C_k \ (k < m)
         \quad \Sigma; \emptyset \vdash M : A}
        {\vdash (d_0, \ldots, d_{m-1};\; M) : A}
\]
where $d_k = \kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N_k)$. A term means a set given a table $T$ that gives each definition its
meaning, $\den{M}_T\rho$; the other forms are interpreted as in
FlatClos, passing $T$ along. A definition means the curried function
of its code, given the table $T_k$ of the definitions older than it
($T_k(j) = T(j)$ for $j < k$, and $T_k(j) = \emptyset$ otherwise):
\[
  \den{f_k}_T\rho = T(k)
  \qquad
  T(k) = \Lambda(\mlam{X} \Lambda(\mlam{Y} \den{N_k}_{T_k}\rho_0[z{:=}X, y{:=}Y]))
\]
\[
  \den{(d_0, \ldots, d_{m-1};\; M)} = \den{M}_T\rho_0
\]
Mon has the syntax of Figure~\ref{fig:grammars}, is typed as GlobalFun,
and has the meaning of its embedding into GlobalFun: an atom and an
operation on atoms are GlobalFun terms already.

\subsection{The Imp Language}

A body runs in a frame of slots, typed by the list
$\Delta = A_0, \ldots, A_{j-1}$ of the types of the slots assigned so
far; slot $x < j$ has type $A_x$. An assignment and a $\kw{case}$ write
the next slot, $|\Delta|$. Atoms and simple expressions are typed as
in Mon, over slots.
\[
  \dfrac{x < |\Delta|}{\Sigma; \Delta \vdash x : A_x}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \Delta \vdash e_1 : A \quad \Sigma; \Delta, A \vdash e_2 : C}
        {\Sigma; \Delta \vdash |\Delta| \mathrel{:=} e_1;\; e_2 : C}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \Delta \vdash a : A + B \quad
         \Sigma; \Delta, A \vdash e_1 : C \quad \Sigma; \Delta, B \vdash e_2 : C}
        {\Sigma; \Delta \vdash \kw{case}\;a\;(|\Delta|.\,e_1)\;(|\Delta|.\,e_2) : C}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \kw{tup}(\Delta_k), A_k \vdash e_k : C_k \ (k < m)
         \quad \Sigma; \emptyset \vdash e : A}
        {\vdash (e_0, \ldots, e_{m-1};\; e) : A}
\]
A definition starts with its environment in slot 0 and its argument in
slot 1. The semantics is in continuation-passing style: the frame
$\sigma$ maps each slot to a set, and the continuation $\kappa$ takes
the meaning of the expression. An atom means $\mathcal{A}\den{x}\sigma =
\sigma(x)$, $\mathcal{A}\den{c}\sigma = \{c\}$, or
$\mathcal{A}\den{f_k}\sigma = T(k)$, and a simple expression
$\mathcal{S}\den{o\;\vec{a}}\sigma$ applies the GlobalFun operator of
$o$ to the meanings of its atoms. An assignment is strict in the value
it assigns, like a $\kw{let}$:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \den{r}_T\sigma\kappa & = & \kappa(\mathcal{S}\den{r}\sigma)\\
  \den{x \mathrel{:=} e_1;\; e_2}_T\sigma\kappa & = &
    \den{e_1}_T\sigma\,(\mlam{D} \Lambda(\mlam{X} \den{e_2}_T\sigma[x{:=}X]\kappa) \app D)\\
  \den{\kw{case}\;a\;(x.\,e_1)\;(x.\,e_2)}_T\sigma\kappa & = &
    \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}\den{a}\sigma,\;
      \mlam{X} \den{e_1}_T\sigma[x{:=}X]\kappa,\;
      \mlam{X} \den{e_2}_T\sigma[x{:=}X]\kappa)\\
  T(k) & = & \Lambda(\mlam{X} \Lambda(\mlam{Y}
    \den{e_k}_{T_k}\rho_0[0{:=}X, 1{:=}Y]\,(\mlam{D} D)))\\
  \den{(e_0, \ldots, e_{m-1};\; e)} & = & \den{e}_T\rho_0\,(\mlam{D} D)
\end{array}
\]

\subsection{The CFG Language}

A tail is typed by the type $R$ of the function body's result, the list
$\vec{\Delta} = \Delta_0, \ldots, \Delta_{j-1}$ of the frames of the
blocks it may jump to, and its own frame $\Delta$. A label $l$ is an
index into $\vec{\Delta}$. Block $t_l$ is typed over the blocks older
than it, so control flow is acyclic. Here $\Delta' \sqsubseteq \Delta$
says that $\Delta'$ is a prefix of $\Delta$; an assignment to slot
$|\Delta'|$ forgets the slots from $|\Delta'|$ on.
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \Delta \vdash r : R}{\Sigma; R; \vec{\Delta}; \Delta \vdash \kw{return}\;r}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Delta' \sqsubseteq \Delta \quad \Sigma; \Delta \vdash r : A \quad
         \Sigma; R; \vec{\Delta}; \Delta', A \vdash t}
        {\Sigma; R; \vec{\Delta}; \Delta \vdash |\Delta'| \mathrel{:=} r;\; t}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Delta_l = \Delta}{\Sigma; R; \vec{\Delta}; \Delta \vdash \kw{goto}\;l}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \Delta \vdash a : A + B \quad \Delta' \sqsubseteq \Delta \quad
         \Delta_{l_1} = \Delta', A \quad \Delta_{l_2} = \Delta', B}
        {\Sigma; R; \vec{\Delta}; \Delta \vdash
         \kw{case}\;a\;(|\Delta'|.\,l_1)\;(|\Delta'|.\,l_2)}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; R; \Delta_0, \ldots, \Delta_{l-1}; \Delta_l \vdash t_l \ (l < j) \quad
         \Sigma; R; \vec{\Delta}; \Delta \vdash t}
        {\Sigma; R; \Delta \vdash (t_0, \ldots, t_{j-1};\; t)}
\]
A definition's function body starts in the frame $\kw{tup}(\Delta_k), A_k$ with
result type $C_k$, and the main function body in the empty frame. A tail means a
set, given its frame $\sigma$ and the meanings $B$ of the blocks it can
see. Writing slot $x$ cuts the frame to the slots before $x$, written
$\sigma|_x$, and then assigns $x$:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \den{\kw{return}\;r}_{T,B}\sigma & = & \mathcal{S}\den{r}\sigma\\
  \den{x \mathrel{:=} r;\; t}_{T,B}\sigma & = &
    \Lambda(\mlam{X} \den{t}_{T,B}\,\sigma|_x[x{:=}X]) \app \mathcal{S}\den{r}\sigma\\
  \den{\kw{goto}\;l}_{T,B}\sigma & = & B(l)(\sigma)\\
  \den{\kw{case}\;a\;(x.\,l_1)\;(x.\,l_2)}_{T,B}\sigma & = &
    \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}\den{a}\sigma,\;
      \mlam{X} B(l_1)(\sigma|_x[x{:=}X]),\;
      \mlam{X} B(l_2)(\sigma|_x[x{:=}X]))
\end{array}
\]
The blocks of a function body $(t_0, \ldots, t_{j-1};\, t)$ mean
$B(l)(\sigma) = \den{t_l}_{T,B_l}\sigma$, where $B_l$ gives the blocks
older than $l$, and the function body means $\den{t}_{T,B}$. Definitions and
programs are given meaning as in Imp, with the function body in place of the
expression and no continuation.

\subsection{The x86$\mu$ Language}

An instruction is typed by the type of the machine state it starts in
and the one it leaves. A state type
$\psi = \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle$ gives the types $\Theta$
of the registers, the frame $\Delta$ of the slots assigned so far, the
open closure $O$ (none, written $\cdot$, or a slot $x$ of $\Delta$ of a
function type), and whether the flag $F$ is set. A register holds a
value of a type $A$, the code $\kw{code}_\alpha(A \rightarrow C)$ or the
environment $\kw{tup}(\alpha)$ of the open closure, or nothing; the
type variable $\alpha$ stands for the hidden types of the environment
of the open closure. A function body declares the size $n$ of its frame. The
operands are typed by these rules, with the atoms typed as in Imp in
the frame of the state:
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \Delta \vdash a : A}{\Sigma; \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle \vdash a : A}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Theta(r) = \tau}{\Sigma; \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle \vdash \%r : \tau}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \psi \vdash a : A[\mu X.\, A / X]}{\Sigma; \psi \vdash \kw{fold}\;a : \mu X.\, A}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Sigma; \psi \vdash a : \mu X.\, A}{\Sigma; \psi \vdash \kw{unfold}\;a : A[\mu X.\, A / X]}
\]
A destination is written at a type $\tau$ and leaves a new state,
$n \vdash d : \psi \Rightarrow_\tau \psi'$. A write to a slot cuts the
frame and closes the open closure:
\[
  n \vdash \%r : \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle \Rightarrow_\tau \langle \Theta[r{:}\tau]; \Delta; O; F \rangle
  \qquad
  \dfrac{\Delta' \sqsubseteq \Delta \quad |\Delta'| < n}
        {n \vdash |\Delta'| : \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle \Rightarrow_A \langle \Theta; \Delta', A; \cdot; F \rangle}
\]
An object $\kw{pair}(a_1, a_2)$ has type $A \rightarrow C$ when
$a_1 : \kw{code}(\Delta; A \rightarrow C)$ and $a_2 : \kw{tup}(\Delta)$;
tuples and injections of operands are typed as in \iswim. With
$\psi = \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle$ and operands typed in
$\psi$, the instructions are typed by:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}c@{}}
  \dfrac{a : \tau \quad n \vdash d : \psi \Rightarrow_\tau \psi'}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{movq}\; a, d : \psi \Rightarrow \psi'}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{a : \kw{Int} \quad \Theta(r) = \kw{Int}}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{subq}\; a, \%r : \psi \Rightarrow \psi}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{a_1 : \kw{Int} \quad a_2 : \kw{Int}}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{cmpq}\; a_2, a_1 : \psi \Rightarrow \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; \kw{set} \rangle}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{n \vdash d : \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; \kw{set} \rangle \Rightarrow_{\kw{Bool}} \psi'}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{sete}\; d : \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; \kw{set} \rangle \Rightarrow \psi'}
  \qquad
  \dfrac{o : A \quad n \vdash d : \psi \Rightarrow_A \psi'}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{alloc}\; o, d : \psi \Rightarrow \psi'}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{a : \kw{tup}(A_1, \ldots, A_m) \quad n \vdash d : \psi \Rightarrow_{A_i} \psi'}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{load}\; \kw{get}_i\; a, d : \psi \Rightarrow \psi'}
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{x : A \rightarrow C \in \Delta}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{fst}\; x, \%r : \psi \Rightarrow
         \langle \mathit{wipe}(\Theta)[r{:}\kw{code}_\alpha(A \rightarrow C)]; \Delta; x; F \rangle}
  \\[14pt]
  \Sigma; n \vdash \kw{snd}\; x, \%r : \langle \Theta; \Delta; x; F \rangle \Rightarrow
    \langle \Theta[r{:}\kw{tup}(\alpha)]; \Delta; x; F \rangle
  \\[14pt]
  \dfrac{a : \kw{code}_\alpha(A \rightarrow C) \quad \Theta(\kw{rdi}) = \kw{tup}(\alpha)
         \quad \Theta(\kw{rsi}) = A}
        {\Sigma; n \vdash \kw{callq}\; a : \psi \Rightarrow \langle \kw{rax}{:}C; \Delta; \cdot; \kw{unset} \rangle}
\end{array}
\]
Here $\mathit{wipe}(\Theta)$ unsets the registers that hold the code or
the environment of an earlier open closure, so $\alpha$ always stands
for the environment of one closure. A sequence $s$ is typed by the result
type $R$, the frames $\vec{\Delta}$ of the blocks it may jump to, and
its starting state:
\[
  \dfrac{\Sigma; n \vdash i : \psi \Rightarrow \psi' \quad \Sigma; n; R; \vec{\Delta} \vdash s : \psi'}
        {\Sigma; n; R; \vec{\Delta} \vdash i;\; s : \psi}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Theta(\kw{rax}) = R}{\Sigma; n; R; \vec{\Delta} \vdash \kw{retq} : \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{\Delta_l = \Delta}{\Sigma; n; R; \vec{\Delta} \vdash \kw{jmp}\;l : \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle}
\]
\[
  \dfrac{x : A + B \in \Delta \quad \Delta' \sqsubseteq \Delta \quad |\Delta'| < n \quad
         \Delta_{l_1} = \Delta', A \quad \Delta_{l_2} = \Delta', B}
        {\Sigma; n; R; \vec{\Delta} \vdash \kw{case}\;x\;(|\Delta'|.\,l_1)\;(|\Delta'|.\,l_2) : \langle \Theta; \Delta; O; F \rangle}
\]
A block starts in the state $\langle \emptyset; \Delta_l; \cdot;
\kw{unset} \rangle$, and blocks are acyclic as in CFG. A definition
$b_k$ of signature $(\Delta_k; A_k \rightarrow C_k)$ starts with its
environment in $\kw{rdi}$ and its argument in $\kw{rsi}$, in the state
$\langle \kw{rdi}{:}\kw{tup}(\Delta_k), \kw{rsi}{:}A_k; \emptyset; \cdot;
\kw{unset} \rangle$, and has result type $C_k$.

The semantics of a sequence is a function of the frame $\sigma$, the
registers $R$ (a map from registers to sets), and the flag $F$ (a set);
an instruction transforms the meaning $\kappa$ of the rest of the
sequence. An unset register and an unset flag hold $\{\langle\rangle\}$.
An operand means $\mathcal{O}\den{a}\sigma R = \mathcal{A}\den{a}\sigma$
for an atom, $R(r)$ for $\%r$, and $\mathcal{O}\den{a}\sigma R$ for
$\kw{fold}\;a$ and $\kw{unfold}\;a$. An object means the operator of its
kind on the meanings of its operands ($\mathsf{pair}$, $\mathcal{T}$,
$\mathsf{inl}$, $\mathsf{inr}$). A write to a destination, written
$\mathit{put}$, updates a register, or binds a slot as an assignment in
CFG does:
\[
  \mathit{put}(\%r, D)\,\kappa\,\sigma R F = \kappa\,\sigma\,R[r{:=}D]\,F
  \qquad
  \mathit{put}(x, D)\,\kappa\,\sigma R F =
    \Lambda(\mlam{X} \kappa\,(\sigma|_x[x{:=}X])\,R\,F) \app D
\]
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \den{\kw{movq}\; a, d}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = & \mathit{put}(d, \mathcal{O}\den{a}\sigma R)\,\kappa\,\sigma R F\\
  \den{\kw{subq}\; a, \%r}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = &
    \kappa\,\sigma\,R[r{:=}\mathsf{prim}_{\kw{sub}}(R(r), \mathcal{O}\den{a}\sigma R)]\,F\\
  \den{\kw{cmpq}\; a_2, a_1}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = &
    \kappa\,\sigma\,R\,(\mathsf{prim}_{\kw{eq}}(\mathcal{O}\den{a_1}\sigma R, \mathcal{O}\den{a_2}\sigma R))\\
  \den{\kw{sete}\; d}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = & \mathit{put}(d, F)\,\kappa\,\sigma R F\\
  \den{\kw{alloc}\; o, d}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = & \mathit{put}(d, \mathcal{O}\den{o}\sigma R)\,\kappa\,\sigma R F\\
  \den{\kw{load}\; \kw{get}_i\; a, d}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = &
    \mathit{put}(d, \pi_{\mathsf{tup}_i}(\mathcal{O}\den{a}\sigma R))\,\kappa\,\sigma R F\\
  \den{\kw{fst}\; x, \%r}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = & \kappa\,\sigma\,R[r{:=}\car(\sigma(x))]\,F\\
  \den{\kw{snd}\; x, \%r}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = & \kappa\,\sigma\,R[r{:=}\cdr(\sigma(x))]\,F\\
  \den{\kw{callq}\; a}\kappa\,\sigma R F & = &
    \mathsf{guard}(C;\; \kappa\,\sigma\,R_C\,\{\langle\rangle\})
    \quad\text{where } C = (\mathcal{O}\den{a}\sigma R \app R(\kw{rdi})) \app R(\kw{rsi})
\end{array}
\]
Here $R_C$ maps $\kw{rax}$ to $C$ and the other registers to
$\{\langle\rangle\}$; only a call can diverge, so only it is strict in
its result. The sequences mean
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \den{i;\; s}_B & = & \den{i}\,\den{s}_B\\
  \den{\kw{retq}}_B\,\sigma R F & = & R(\kw{rax})\\
  \den{\kw{jmp}\;l}_B\,\sigma R F & = & B(l)(\sigma)\\
  \den{\kw{case}\;x\;(y.\,l_1)\;(y.\,l_2)}_B\,\sigma R F & = &
    \mathcal{C}(\sigma(x),\;
      \mlam{X} B(l_1)(\sigma|_y[y{:=}X]),\;
      \mlam{X} B(l_2)(\sigma|_y[y{:=}X]))
\end{array}
\]
where a block means $B(l)(\sigma) = \den{s_l}_{B_l}\,\sigma\,R_0\,
\{\langle\rangle\}$, with $R_0$ mapping every register to
$\{\langle\rangle\}$ and $B_l$ giving the blocks older than $l$. A
definition means
$T(k) = \Lambda(\mlam{X} \Lambda(\mlam{Y} \den{s}_B\,\rho_0\,R_{X,Y}\,
\{\langle\rangle\}))$ for its function body $s$ with blocks $B$, where
$R_{X,Y}$ maps $\kw{rdi}$ to $X$, $\kw{rsi}$ to $Y$, and $\kw{rax}$ to
$\{\langle\rangle\}$; the tables are as in GlobalFun. A program means
the meaning of its main function body in $\rho_0$ and $R_0$, with the flag
$\{\langle\rangle\}$.

\section{The Compiler Passes}
\label{app:passes}

%% From definitions.tex (Compilation) and agda/Mu/Compiler/*.agda.
This appendix defines each pass of Section~\ref{sec:compiler} as a
recursive function on the syntax of Figure~\ref{fig:grammars}. In each
pass, the clauses not shown are homomorphic: they translate the
subterms and rebuild the same form. No pass inspects a recursive type;
$\kw{fold}$ and $\kw{unfold}$ pass through every pass as operations
with one operand. The compiler is the composition of the passes:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
  \pass{compile} = \pass{select} \circ \pass{explicate} \circ \pass{lift}
    \circ \pass{rco} \circ \pass{globalize}\\
  \qquad\qquad {} \circ \pass{delay} \circ \pass{concretize}_\emptyset
    \circ \pass{optimize} \circ \pass{enclose} \circ \pass{simplify}
\end{array}
\]

\subsection{\pass{simplify} (\iswim\ to \iswim)}

%% agda/Mu/Compiler/Simplify.agda: Info, Known, self, info, under,
%% known-as, value, small, simp, simp-app, simp-call, simp-let, simp-get,
%% simp-case, simp-prim, simp-unfold, inline-fuel, inline-size.
On a term, \pass{simplify} returns a pair $(T \mid K)$ of the simplified
term $T$ and what it knows about the term's value, $K$: a value $V$, a
function $\langle \lambda x.\, N, E \rangle$ (its body before
simplification, with what was known where it was defined), or nothing,
$\bot$. An environment $E$ maps each variable to such a pair: the term
that replaces the variable and what the variable is known to be. Values
are variables, $\lambda$-abstractions, constants, and tuples,
injections, and $\kw{fold}$s of values. A value is \emph{small} when it
is a variable, a constant, $\langle\rangle$, or $\kw{inl}\;a$ or
$\kw{inr}\;a$ of one of those; only small values are duplicated. We
write $\mathit{self}(V) = (V \mid V)$, and $\mathit{info}(M) = (M \mid
M)$ if $M$ is a value and $(M \mid \bot)$ otherwise. Under a binder $x$
that stays, $x$ is itself and is known only as itself:
$E' = E[x{:=}\mathit{self}(x)]$.

The pass carries fuel $\phi$ that limits nested inlining. Every
equation passes $\phi$ along unchanged except the one for a call,
which inlines with one less:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{simp}_E(x) & = & E(x)\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\lambda x.\, N) & = &
    (\lambda x.\, T \mid \langle \lambda x.\, N, E \rangle)\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (T \mid K) = \pass{simp}_{E[x:=\mathit{self}(x)]}(N)\\
  \pass{simp}_E(L\;M) & = & \pass{app}_E(L, \pass{simp}_E(M))\\
  \pass{simp}_E(c) & = & \mathit{self}(c)\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\kw{tuple}\;(M_1, \ldots, M_n)) & = &
    \mathit{info}(\kw{tuple}\;(T_1, \ldots, T_n))\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (T_i \mid K_i) = \pass{simp}_E(M_i)\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\kw{get}_i\,L) & = & \pass{get}_i(\pass{simp}_E(L))\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\kw{inl}\;M) & = & \mathit{info}(\kw{inl}\;T)
    \quad \text{where } (T \mid K) = \pass{simp}_E(M)\\
  & & \quad \text{and likewise for } \kw{inr} \text{ and } \kw{fold}\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\kw{case}\;L\;(x.\,M)\;(y.\,N)) & = &
    \pass{case}_E(\pass{simp}_E(L), x.\,M, y.\,N)\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\kw{prim}_p\;L\;M) & = & \pass{prim}_p(\pass{simp}_E(L), \pass{simp}_E(M))\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\kw{let}\;x = M\;\kw{in}\;N) & = & \pass{let}_E(x.\,N, \pass{simp}_E(M))\\
  \pass{simp}_E(\kw{unfold}\;L) & = & \pass{unfold}(\pass{simp}_E(L))
\end{array}
\]
An application of a $\lambda$-abstraction is a $\beta$-redex; any other
application is a call, which inlines a known function when there is
fuel left and its body, simplified at the call, has at most 24 nodes:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{app}_E(\lambda x.\, N, I) & = & \pass{let}_E(x.\,N, I)\\
  \pass{app}_E(L, I) & = & \pass{call}(\pass{simp}_E(L), I)
    \quad \text{otherwise}\\[4pt]
  \pass{call}^{\phi+1}((T \mid \langle \lambda x.\, N, E_f \rangle), I) & = & P
    \quad \text{if } |P| \leq 24, \text{ where } P = \pass{let}^{\phi}_{E_f}(x.\,N, I)\\
  & = & (T\;T' \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{otherwise, where } I = (T' \mid K')\\
  \pass{call}^{\phi}((T \mid K), (T' \mid K')) & = & (T\;T' \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{in all other cases}
\end{array}
\]
Here $|P|$ is the number of nodes in the term of $P$. A body bound to an
operand, by $\kw{let}$, $\beta$, or inlining, substitutes the operand
if it is small, and otherwise keeps it bound by a $\kw{let}$:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{let}_E(x.\,N, (V \mid K)) & = & \pass{simp}_{E[x:=(V \mid K)]}(N)
    \quad \text{if } V \text{ is small}\\
  & = & (\kw{let}\;x = V\;\kw{in}\;T \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{if } V \text{ is another value,}\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (T \mid K'') = \pass{simp}_{E[x:=(x \mid K)]}(N)\\
  & = & (\kw{let}\;x = V\;\kw{in}\;T \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{otherwise,}\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (T \mid K'') = \pass{simp}_{E[x:=\mathit{self}(x)]}(N)
\end{array}
\]
A projection, $\kw{unfold}$, $\kw{case}$, or primitive on a known value
reduces:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{get}_i((T \mid \kw{tuple}\;(K_1, \ldots, K_n))) & = & \mathit{self}(K_i)
    \quad \text{if } K_i \text{ is small}\\
  & = & (\kw{get}_i\,T \mid K_i) \quad \text{otherwise}\\
  \pass{get}_i((T \mid K)) & = & (\kw{get}_i\,T \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{if } K \text{ is not a tuple}\\
  \pass{unfold}((T \mid \kw{fold}\;K)) & = & \mathit{self}(K)
    \quad \text{if } K \text{ is small}\\
  & = & (\kw{unfold}\;T \mid K) \quad \text{otherwise}\\
  \pass{unfold}((T \mid K)) & = & (\kw{unfold}\;T \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{if } K \text{ is not a } \kw{fold}\\
  \pass{case}_E((T \mid \kw{inl}\;K), x.\,M, y.\,N) & = & \pass{simp}_{E[x:=\mathit{self}(K)]}(M)
    \quad \text{if } K \text{ is small}\\
  & & \quad \text{(and likewise for } \kw{inr}\text{)}\\
  \pass{case}_E((T \mid K), x.\,M, y.\,N) & = &
    (\kw{case}\;T\;(x.\,T_1)\;(y.\,T_2) \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{otherwise,}\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (T_1 \mid K_1) = \pass{simp}_{E[x:=\mathit{self}(x)]}(M)\\
  & & \quad \text{and } (T_2 \mid K_2) = \pass{simp}_{E[y:=\mathit{self}(y)]}(N)\\
  \pass{prim}_p((T_1 \mid n), (T_2 \mid m)) & = & \mathit{self}(\mathit{res}(\delta(p, n, m)))
    \quad \text{if } n \text{ and } m \text{ are integers}\\
  \pass{prim}_p((T_1 \mid K_1), (T_2 \mid K_2)) & = & (\kw{prim}_p\;T_1\;T_2 \mid \bot)
    \quad \text{otherwise}
\end{array}
\]
Here $\mathit{res}(k) = k$ for an integer $k$,
$\mathit{res}(\mathsf{true}) = \kw{inl}\;\langle\rangle$, and
$\mathit{res}(\mathsf{false}) = \kw{inr}\;\langle\rangle$. Finally,
\[
  \pass{simplify}(M) = T
  \quad \text{where } (T \mid K) = \pass{simp}^{4}_{E_0}(M)
  \text{ and } E_0(x) = \mathit{self}(x) \text{ for every } x.
\]
\pass{simplify} drops only evaluations whose value is known, which
have no effect, and otherwise keeps the order of evaluation: an operand
that is not a value is never substituted but stays bound by a
$\kw{let}$.

\subsection{\pass{enclose} (\iswim\ to Clos)}

\pass{enclose} makes each function a closure that captures every
variable in scope, in order, so its body is unchanged:
\[
  \pass{enclose}(\lambda y.\, N) =
    \kw{clos}\;(x_1, \ldots, x_k, y.\,\pass{enclose}(N))\;(x_1, \ldots, x_k)
\]
where $x_1, \ldots, x_k$ are the variables in scope.

\subsection{\pass{optimize} (Clos to Clos)}

\pass{optimize} removes the captured values that the code does not use.
Closures are strict, so a captured term is dropped only when the code
does not use its name and the term is a variable, which is all that
\pass{enclose} captures:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
  \pass{optimize}(\kw{clos}\;(x_1, \ldots, x_n, y.\,N)\;(M_1, \ldots, M_n)) =\\
  \qquad \kw{clos}\;(x_{i_1}, \ldots, x_{i_k}, y.\,\pass{optimize}(N))\;
    (\pass{optimize}(M_{i_1}), \ldots, \pass{optimize}(M_{i_k}))\\
  \qquad \text{where } \{ i_1 < \cdots < i_k \} =
    \{ i \mid x_i \in \mathit{FV}(N) \text{ or } M_i \text{ is not a variable} \}
\end{array}
\]

\subsection{\pass{concretize} (Clos to EnvClos)}

Each closure stores its captured values in one tuple, bound to $z$ in
its code, and the code reads the name $x_i$ as $\kw{get}_i\,z$. The map
$\varphi$ sends each Clos variable to a reference, $x$ or
$\kw{get}_i\,z$, in EnvClos:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
  \pass{concretize}_\varphi(x) = \varphi(x)\\
  \pass{concretize}_\varphi(\kw{clos}\;(x_1, \ldots, x_n, y.\,N)\;(M_1, \ldots, M_n)) =\\
  \qquad \kw{clos}\;(z, y.\,\pass{concretize}_{\varphi'}(N))\;
      (\pass{concretize}_\varphi(M_1), \ldots, \pass{concretize}_\varphi(M_n))\\
  \qquad \text{where } \varphi' =
    \{ y \mapsto y,\; x_1 \mapsto \kw{get}_1\,z,\; \ldots,\; x_n \mapsto \kw{get}_n\,z \}
\end{array}
\]
Under a $\kw{let}$ or $\kw{case}$ the bound variable maps to itself,
and a program is concretized with the empty map.

\subsection{\pass{delay} (EnvClos to FlatClos)}

A closure becomes a pair of its code and the tuple of its captured
values, and an application opens the closure and calls its code:
\[
  \pass{delay}(\kw{clos}\;(z, y.\,N)\;\vec{M}) =
    \kw{pack}\;(\kw{code}\;(z, y.\,\pass{delay}(N)))\;(\kw{tuple}\;\pass{delay}(\vec{M}))
\]
\[
  \pass{delay}(L\;M) = \kw{callc}\;\pass{delay}(L)\;\pass{delay}(M)
\]

\subsection{\pass{globalize} (FlatClos to GlobalFun)}

The code of every function becomes a global definition, and the code
itself a reference to it. The pass threads the list $\vec{d}$ of the
definitions made so far, from left to right. For code, it first
globalizes the body, so the functions nested in it become definitions
first. For any other form $\mathit{op}$ with subterms $A_1, \ldots,
A_n$ (each under its binders $\bar{x}_i$):
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{glob}(x, \vec{d}) & = & (x, \vec{d})\\
  \pass{glob}(\kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N), \vec{d}) & = &
    (f_{|\vec{d}_1|},\; (\vec{d}_1, \kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N')))\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (N', \vec{d}_1) = \pass{glob}(N, \vec{d})\\
  \pass{glob}(\mathit{op}(\bar{x}_1.\,A_1, \ldots, \bar{x}_n.\,A_n), \vec{d}_0) & = &
    (\mathit{op}(\bar{x}_1.\,A'_1, \ldots, \bar{x}_n.\,A'_n), \vec{d}_n)\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (A'_i, \vec{d}_i) = \pass{glob}(A_i, \vec{d}_{i-1})\\[4pt]
  \pass{globalize}(M) & = & (\vec{d};\; M')
    \quad \text{where } (M', \vec{d}) = \pass{glob}(M, \epsilon)
\end{array}
\]
Each definition refers only to the definitions made before it.

\subsection{\pass{remove complex operands} (GlobalFun to Mon)}

Every complex operand is bound to a fresh variable by a $\kw{let}$,
from left to right, so the operands of every operation become atoms. A
$\kw{case}$ or $\kw{let}$ is complex; its branches and body are
translated in place, so nothing is duplicated. An operand that is an
atom stays in place; any other operand is translated and bound to a
fresh variable:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{atomize}(a) & = & (\epsilon;\; a)\\
  \pass{atomize}(M) & = & (x{=}\pass{rco}(M);\; x)
    \quad \text{otherwise } (x \text{ fresh})\\
  \pass{atomize}(M_1, \ldots, M_n) & = & (\vec{b}_1 \cdots \vec{b}_n;\; a_1, \ldots, a_n)
    \quad \text{where } (\vec{b}_i; a_i) = \pass{atomize}(M_i)\\
  \pass{lets}(x_1{=}N_1 \cdots x_k{=}N_k,\; N) & = &
    \kw{let}\;x_1 = N_1\;\kw{in}\;\cdots\;\kw{let}\;x_k = N_k\;\kw{in}\;N\\[4pt]
  \pass{rco}(a) & = & a\\
  \pass{rco}(o\;(M_1, \ldots, M_n)) & = & \pass{lets}(\vec{b},\; o\;(a_1, \ldots, a_n))\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (\vec{b}; a_1, \ldots, a_n) = \pass{atomize}(M_1, \ldots, M_n)\\
  \pass{rco}(\kw{case}\;L\;(x.\,M)\;(y.\,N)) & = &
    \pass{lets}(\vec{b},\; \kw{case}\;a\;(x.\,\pass{rco}(M))\;(y.\,\pass{rco}(N)))\\
  & & \quad \text{where } (\vec{b}; a) = \pass{atomize}(L)\\
  \pass{rco}(\kw{let}\;x = M\;\kw{in}\;N) & = & \kw{let}\;x = \pass{rco}(M)\;\kw{in}\;\pass{rco}(N)\\
  \pass{rco}(\kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N)) & = & \kw{code}\;(z, y.\,\pass{rco}(N))\\
  \pass{rco}((d_0, \ldots, d_{m-1};\; M)) & = & (\pass{rco}(d_0), \ldots, \pass{rco}(d_{m-1});\; \pass{rco}(M))
\end{array}
\]
where $o$ ranges over the operations of Mon (Figure~\ref{fig:grammars}).

\subsection{\pass{lift locals} (Mon to Imp)}

A $\kw{let}$ becomes an assignment to the next slot of the frame, and
the variable a $\kw{case}$ binds becomes the slot its payload is
written to. A variable becomes the slot at its level $\ell(x)$, the
number of variables bound before it; in a definition
$\kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N)$, $\ell(z) = 0$ and $\ell(y) = 1$. With $\Gamma$
the variables in scope:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{lift}(x) & = & \ell(x)\\
  \pass{lift}(\kw{let}\;x = M\;\kw{in}\;N) & = &
    |\Gamma| \mathrel{:=} \pass{lift}(M);\; \pass{lift}(N)\\
  \pass{lift}(\kw{case}\;a\;(x.\,M)\;(y.\,N)) & = &
    \kw{case}\;\pass{lift}(a)\;(|\Gamma|.\,\pass{lift}(M))\;(|\Gamma|.\,\pass{lift}(N))\\
  \pass{lift}(\kw{code}\;(z, y.\,N)) & = & \pass{lift}(N)\\
  \pass{lift}((d_0, \ldots, d_{m-1};\; M)) & = &
    (\pass{lift}(d_0), \ldots, \pass{lift}(d_{m-1});\; \pass{lift}(M))
\end{array}
\]
and every other form is a simple expression, unchanged.

\subsection{\pass{explicate control} (Imp to CFG)}

$\pass{tail}(e)$ translates $e$ in tail position, and
$\pass{assign}(e, x, k)$ translates ``write $e$ to slot $x$, then run
the tail $k$'' in continuation-passing style. A $\kw{case}$ on the
right of an assignment adds $k$ as a block, the join point, to which
both branches jump. $\pass{add}(t)$ adds the block $t$ and returns its
label. Both functions thread the blocks made so far, each typed over
the older ones, so a label made earlier stays valid as more blocks are
added. Blocks are added in the order in which the equations compute
them, inner calls first and from left to right: $\pass{tail}(e_2)$
before the assignment of $e_1$, $l$ before $l_1$, and $l_1$ before
$l_2$.
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{tail}(r) & = & \kw{return}\;r\\
  \pass{tail}(x \mathrel{:=} e_1;\; e_2) & = & \pass{assign}(e_1, x, \pass{tail}(e_2))\\
  \pass{tail}(\kw{case}\;a\;(x.\,e_1)\;(x.\,e_2)) & = & \kw{case}\;a\;(x.\,l_1)\;(x.\,l_2)
    \quad \text{where } l_i = \pass{add}(\pass{tail}(e_i))\\[4pt]
  \pass{assign}(r, x, k) & = & x \mathrel{:=} r;\; k\\
  \pass{assign}(x' \mathrel{:=} e_1;\; e_2, x, k) & = & \pass{assign}(e_1, x', \pass{assign}(e_2, x, k))\\
  \pass{assign}(\kw{case}\;a\;(x'.\,e_1)\;(x'.\,e_2), x, k) & = & \kw{case}\;a\;(x'.\,l_1)\;(x'.\,l_2)\\
  & & \quad \text{where } l = \pass{add}(k) \text{ and } l_i = \pass{add}(\pass{assign}(e_i, x, \kw{goto}\;l))\\[4pt]
  \pass{explicate}(e) & = & (\vec{t};\; t)
    \quad \text{where } t = \pass{tail}(e) \text{ and } \vec{t} \text{ are the blocks it adds}
\end{array}
\]
and $\pass{explicate}$ maps over the definitions and main expression of
a program.

\subsection{\pass{select instructions} (CFG to x86$\mu$)}

The code of a simple expression $r$ is selected in continuation-passing
style, $\pass{sel}(r, o, k)$: its value goes to $o$, which is either
$\kw{rax}$ (written $\kw{ret}$) or the next slot $x$ (written
$\kw{to}\;x$), and then the instructions $k$ run. We write $\bar{o}$
for the destination, $\%\kw{rax}$ or $x$. An operand of a function
type or a sum type is a slot $x$, because no constant has those types,
so $\kw{callc}$ and $\kw{case}$ take a slot.
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \pass{sel}(a, o, k) & = & \kw{movq}\; a, \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{pack}\;a_1\;a_2, o, k) & = & \kw{alloc}\; \kw{pair}(a_1, a_2), \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{callc}\;x\;a, o, k) & = &
    \kw{fst}\; x, \%\kw{rax};\; \kw{snd}\; x, \%\kw{rdi};\; \kw{movq}\; a, \%\kw{rsi};\\
  & & \kw{callq}\; \%\kw{rax};\; \kw{movq}\; \%\kw{rax}, \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{tuple}\;(a_1, \ldots, a_n), o, k) & = & \kw{alloc}\; \kw{tuple}(a_1, \ldots, a_n), \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{get}_i\,a, o, k) & = & \kw{load}\; \kw{get}_i\; a, \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{inl}\;a, o, k) & = & \kw{alloc}\; \kw{inl}(a), \bar{o};\; k
    \qquad \text{and likewise for } \kw{inr}\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{prim}_{\kw{sub}}\;a_1\;a_2, o, k) & = &
    \kw{movq}\; a_1, \%\kw{rax};\; \kw{subq}\; a_2, \%\kw{rax};\; \kw{movq}\; \%\kw{rax}, \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{prim}_{\kw{eq}}\;a_1\;a_2, o, k) & = & \kw{cmpq}\; a_2, a_1;\; \kw{sete}\; \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{fold}\;a, o, k) & = & \kw{movq}\; \kw{fold}\;a, \bar{o};\; k\\
  \pass{sel}(\kw{unfold}\;a, o, k) & = & \kw{movq}\; \kw{unfold}\;a, \bar{o};\; k\\[4pt]
  \pass{tail}(\kw{return}\;r) & = & \pass{sel}(r, \kw{ret}, \kw{retq})\\
  \pass{tail}(x \mathrel{:=} r;\; t) & = & \pass{sel}(r, \kw{to}\;x, \pass{tail}(t))\\
  \pass{tail}(\kw{goto}\;l) & = & \kw{jmp}\; l\\
  \pass{tail}(\kw{case}\;x\;(y.\,l_1)\;(y.\,l_2)) & = & \kw{case}\;x\;(y.\,l_1)\;(y.\,l_2)
\end{array}
\]
The frame of a function body has as many slots as the largest frame
that any of its tails starts in or writes. With $\Delta$ the frame a tail starts in:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}
  \mathit{depth}(\kw{return}\;r) = \mathit{depth}(\kw{goto}\;l) & = & |\Delta|\\
  \mathit{depth}(x \mathrel{:=} r;\; t) & = & \max(|\Delta|, \mathit{depth}(t))\\
  \mathit{depth}(\kw{case}\;a\;(x.\,l_1)\;(x.\,l_2)) & = & \max(|\Delta|, x + 1)\\[4pt]
  \pass{select}((t_0, \ldots, t_{j-1};\; t)) & = &
    (n;\; \pass{tail}(t_0), \ldots, \pass{tail}(t_{j-1});\; \pass{tail}(t))\\
  \pass{select}^{+}((t_0, \ldots, t_{j-1};\; t)) & = &
    (n;\; \pass{tail}(t_0), \ldots, \pass{tail}(t_{j-1});\\
  & & \quad \kw{movq}\; \%\kw{rdi}, 0;\; \kw{movq}\; \%\kw{rsi}, 1;\; \pass{tail}(t))
\end{array}
\]
with $n = \max(\mathit{depth}(t_0), \ldots, \mathit{depth}(t_{j-1}),
\mathit{depth}(t))$. The main function body uses $\pass{select}$, and
each definition uses $\pass{select}^{+}$, whose prologue moves the
environment and argument from $\kw{rdi}$ and $\kw{rsi}$ to slots 0 and
1: $\pass{select}((b_0, \ldots, b_{m-1};\; b)) =
(\pass{select}^{+}(b_0), \ldots, \pass{select}^{+}(b_{m-1});\;
\pass{select}(b))$.
