\subsection{Adequacy}
\label{sec:adequacy}

Theorem~\ref{thm:compiler} is stated in terms of denotations. To
connect it to running code, we prove that the graph-model semantics of
\iswim\ and of x86$\mu$ are adequate with respect to the clocked
interpreters of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} (Appendix~\ref{app:interp}).

\begin{theorem}[Adequacy]\label{thm:adequacy}
Let $M$ be a closed term of \iswim. For every constant $c$,
$c \in \den{M}$ if and only if the interpreter of \iswim\ terminates on
$M$ with $c$, and $\den{M}$ is empty if and only if the interpreter
diverges on $M$, that is, runs out of clock for every clock. The same
holds for x86$\mu$ and the program $\pass{compile}(M)$.
\end{theorem}

The proof for each language has two halves. Soundness says that what
the interpreter returns at some clock denotes a part of the meaning of
the program. Adequacy says that every element of the meaning is
related to the value that the interpreter returns from some clock on,
by a logical relation defined by recursion on the element, as in
earlier adequacy proofs for graph and filter models
\citep{Siek:2017ab,Wadler:2020aa}. Together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:compiler}, Theorem~\ref{thm:adequacy} gives the
theorem that the operational proofs state: for a program of integer
type, the interpreters of \iswim\ and x86$\mu$ terminate with the same
integer, or both diverge. The adequacy proofs are 1,529 lines of Agda.
We report them separately from the comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}, because with the graph-model semantics
taken as the definition of the languages, the proof of the compiler is
complete without them.
