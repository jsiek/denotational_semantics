\section{Adequacy}
\label{sec:adequacy}

Each of our five correctness results speaks of a different semantics.
To check that our mechanized semantics agree, we prove the graph model
adequate with respect to the clocked interpreters, the CEK machines,
and the reduction semantics (Appendices~\ref{app:interp},
\ref{app:machines}, and~\ref{app:reduction}), and we prove the
Scott-domain semantics adequate with respect to the clocked
interpreters. We regard these proofs as checks on our definitions, not
as contributions, because each one adapts a textbook proof. We state
the proposition for the interpreters.

\begin{proposition}[Adequacy]\label{prop:adequacy}
Let $M$ be a closed term of \fpc. For every constant $c$,
$c \in \den{M}$ if and only if the interpreter of \fpc\ terminates on
$M$ with $c$, and $\den{M}$ is empty if and only if the interpreter
diverges on $M$, that is, times out at every clock. The same holds
for \instr\ and $\pass{compile}(M)$. In the Scott-domain semantics,
$\den{M}\rho_0 = \up{(c, ())}$ if and only if the interpreter
terminates on $M$ with $c$, and $\den{M}\rho_0 = \bot$ if and only if
the interpreter diverges on $M$, and the same holds for \instr.
\end{proposition}

The graph-model half of the proposition also holds with the CEK machines
of \fpc\ and \instr\ in place of the interpreters, and with the
reduction semantics of \fpc. These cover every semantics in our
comparison, because the reduction-semantics proof of correctness uses
the CEK machine of \instr\ as its target.

Each proof has two halves. Soundness says that if the operational
semantics returns a value, then that value denotes a part of the
meaning of the program. Adequacy says that every element of the
meaning is related to the value that the operational semantics
returns, by a logical relation. For the reduction semantics, we follow
\citet{Plotkin:1977oc}. Each reduction step preserves the meaning of
a term, by a substitution lemma, and the logical relation relates
elements to closed values. For the interpreters and the CEK machines,
the logical relation relates elements to values with environments and
is defined by recursion on the element, as in earlier adequacy proofs
for graph and filter models \citep{Siek:2017ab,Wadler:2020aa}. For the
machines, soundness gives a meaning to each continuation and shows
that a step never enlarges the meaning of the machine state. For Scott
domains, the proof follows the textbook approach
\citep{Plotkin:1985aa,Winskel:1993uq,Pitts:1996aa}. The logical
relation occurs negatively at recursive types, and we define it by
recursion on the stages of the bilimit, as for \pass{split closure}
(Section~\ref{sec:scott-delay}). It relates each approximation of a
domain element to an interpreter value.

Together with Theorems~\ref{thm:compiler} and~\ref{thm:scott},
Proposition~\ref{prop:adequacy} gives the statements of the operational
correctness results
(Propositions~\ref{prop:interp}--\ref{prop:reduction}), namely that for
a program of integer type the source and the target terminate with the
same integer or both diverge. All five correctness results are
therefore equivalent. For the graph model, the adequacy proofs take
1,529 lines of Agda for the interpreters, 1,606 for the CEK machines,
and 1,574 for the reduction semantics, and the proof for Scott domains
takes 1,886 lines. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} explains why we report
them separately.
