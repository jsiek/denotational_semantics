Our technical contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A proof, mechanized in Agda, that a ten-pass compiler from a
  call-by-value $\lambda$-calculus with iso-recursive types, through
  closure conversion, to a typed assembly language is correct, using a
  denotational (graph-model) semantics for each language
  (\S\ref{sec:compiler-proof}).
\item Approximant logical relations relate two denotations by
  recursion on the depth of a finite approximation of one of them,
  which plays the key role in proving correctness for the lowering of
  closures to their flat representation (\S\ref{sec:approx-lr}).
\item Proofs that the denotational semantics of the source language
  and of the assembly language are adequate with respect to
  interpreters for those languages, which provides assurance that our
  correctness theorem is equivalent to one based on operational
  semantics (\S\ref{sec:adequacy}).
\item A comparison with proofs of correctness for the same compiler
  using three different operational semantics (abstract machines,
  clocked interpreters and reduction semantics), which shows that the
  denotational proofs are among the smallest when program values stay
  at the same level of abstraction and are the largest when values are
  lowered (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}).
\end{itemize}
