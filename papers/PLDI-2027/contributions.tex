\begin{itemize}
\item A denotational proof that a compiler from a call-by-value
  $\lambda$-calculus with iso-recursive types, through closure
  conversion, to a typed assembly language is correct in the strong
  sense that divergence is both preserved and reflected, using a
  graph-model semantics for each language and mechanized in Agda
  (\S\ref{sec:compiler-proof}).
\item Approximant logical relations relate two denotations by
  recursion on the depth of a finite approximation of one of them,
  which is what lets us prove that lowering closures to their flat
  representation reflects divergence, the direction that earlier
  denotational proofs of defunctionalization left open
  (\S\ref{sec:approx-lr}).
\item Proofs that the denotational semantics of the source language
  and of the assembly language are adequate with respect to
  interpreters for those languages, which turns our theorem about
  denotations into an end-to-end theorem about running code, the same
  one that operational semantics would give (\S\ref{sec:adequacy}).
\item A comparison, on one ten-pass compiler, of the size of
  mechanized correctness proofs in the denotational style and in three
  operational styles (abstract machines, clocked interpreters and
  reduction semantics), which shows that the denotational proofs are
  among the smallest when program values stay at the same level of
  abstraction and are the largest when values are lowered
  (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}).
\end{itemize}
