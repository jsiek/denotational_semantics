\begin{itemize}
\item A proof that closure conversion preserves and reflects a
  denotational semantics for a call-by-value $\lambda$-calculus with
  iso-recursive types. We extend this proof to the whole compiler,
  targeting typed assembly language, and we mechanize the proof in
  Agda (\S\ref{sec:compiler-proof}).
\item Approximant logical relations relate two denotations by
  recursion on the depth of a finite set of graph elements, which lets
  us prove semantic reflection for lowering closures to their flat
  representation, the direction that guarantees that diverging programs
  compile to diverging programs and that earlier denotational proofs
  left open (\S\ref{sec:approx-lr}).
\item Proofs that the denotational semantics of the source and of the
  assembly language are adequate with respect to the interpreters,
  which connect our whole-compiler theorem about denotations to the
  corresponding theorems about operational semantics
  (\S\ref{sec:adequacy}).
\item A comparison, on a ten-pass compiler, of the size of mechanized
  correctness proofs in two denotational styles (graph model and
  Scott domains) and three operational styles (abstract machines,
  clocked interpreters and reduction semantics), each against its own semantics: the graph-model proofs are the smallest of the five for \pass{simplify}, \pass{globalize}, and \pass{explicate control}, while for \pass{delay} both denotational proofs are several times larger than the operational ones (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}).
\end{itemize}
